# Lens-model recording study — jsPsych, DataPipe, Google Drive

Open this folder in VS Code and serve it with Live Server. For a local dry run, open:

`http://127.0.0.1:5500/index.html?rid=TEST001&task_order=A,B,C&first_A=low&first_B=high&first_C=low`

`rid` is the Qualtrics ResponseID. A=Add-N, B=button pressing, C=anagrams. Qualtrics can set `task_order` to one permutation of `A,B,C` and each `first_A`, `first_B`, `first_C` to `high` or `low`. If the optional order parameters are missing, this code randomizes them. For participants, host the static files on HTTPS and send the URL from Qualtrics; localhost works only on your own computer.

## Practice, camera check, and recorded tasks

Before opening the camera, participants complete **three unrecorded practice trials for each task** (nine in total). The practice runs Add-0 once and Add-3 twice, button targets of 10, 60 and 10, and one three-letter plus two seven-letter anagrams. Before the first practice trial of each task, participants see a full instruction screen explaining timing, responses, examples, and the applicable effort levels. Each practice trial lasts 10 seconds and gives task feedback; it requires no effort rating or video upload. Practice rows are labeled `phase=practice` in the participant CSV and have blank `video_filename` and `effort` cells. They are followed by the camera checklist and baseline video, then the 24 recorded and effort-rated trials. A separate attention check appears after trial 12; a final honesty question appears after trial 24. Both answers are saved as ordinary CSV rows.

Each participant completes three eight-trial blocks. Within a task, four high trials and four low trials appear in counterbalanced order, as specified by Qualtrics. Each active trial lasts 10 seconds. Add-0/3 shows one digit from a four-digit number at each of seconds 1, 2, 3, and 4; at second 5 the number disappears and a four-digit response box appears until second 10. Add 0 or 3 to each digit, retaining only the last digit of sums above 9 (so 9+3 becomes 2). Button pressing targets 10 or 60 presses in 10 seconds and the button disappears as soon as the target is met. Anagrams use four three-letter words or four seven-letter words; each submitted guess receives immediate correctness feedback and further guesses are allowed until time expires. A result screen stays visible while that trial’s video uploads. The participant CSV contains ordinary columns for digits and answer, press count and target time, anagram guesses and accuracy, trial order, effort rating, and video filename. Task-specific columns are blank on other task rows; no JSON text is stored in a CSV cell. Read `study.js` to modify stimuli or instructions.

The camera screen displays a live preview with a face-centering oval and checks actual resolution of at least 320×240. The participant confirms five checklist items: centered clear face; head and shoulders visible; adequate light; one person; commitment to maintain framing on every trial. It records a five-second neutral baseline. Each subsequent screen repeats the framing reminder, then records a separate webcam video during the ten-second task. The script loads an on-device MediaPipe face detector after camera permission. During the neutral baseline and recorded trials, it samples roughly every 0.5 seconds. After two missing-face samples or any sample with multiple faces, the baseline pauses until the face returns. During a task trial, a face alert interrupts the attempt, discards its incomplete video and responses locally, waits until exactly one face is visible, and restarts the entire 10-second trial using the same stimulus. Only the completed attempt is uploaded and scored; `face_restart_count` and `interrupted_active_ms` record discarded attempts on the final task CSV row. A trial cannot start until one face is detected, and face detection must load before recording begins. The CSV also records `face_pause_ms`, `face_alert_count`, `face_samples`, and `face_monitor_status` for the completed attempt. It does **not** assess lighting, shoulders, identity, or continuous face presence between samples; face detection can miss a face. If the model or CDN fails to load, recording stops with an explanatory error; the participant does not silently proceed without face-based pausing. The face monitor does not upload camera frames; only the separately consented trial videos are uploaded. The camera stays active across the study; each uploaded recording starts at the successful task attempt and stops at its end. No audio is recorded.

## Where files go

The experiment ID in `study.js` is `ZA4MQr5H87Iy`. The jsPsych DataPipe extension uses `stream: true`: it saves a participant CSV at completion and stages completed rows during the study. If someone leaves early, DataPipe recovers a `.partial.json` backup containing their completed trials. The regular completed dataset is still a CSV with flat columns; the JSON file exists only as an incomplete-session backup. Each baseline or trial clip uploads as its own `.webm` or `.mp4` through DataPipe's base64 endpoint to the **Google Drive folder connected to that DataPipe experiment ID**. Filenames share a randomly generated `subject_id` for joining videos to CSV rows. Every task row has `effort` (1–7), `trial_key` (the filename without extension), and `video_filename` (the exact `.webm` or `.mp4` name). Example: `trial_key` = `a1b2..._trial_07_A_high`, `video_filename` = `a1b2..._trial_07_A_high.webm`. The task name, condition, and trial number are also separate columns. Baseline has its own row. Videos advance on HTTP 201 (stored) or 202 (queued). The page holds an unsent recording in browser memory while an upload needs retry; participants must keep the tab open. The limit is set to 18 MB per video to stay below DataPipe's 32 MB request ceiling.

On the DataPipe dashboard enable **Accept new data** and **Accept base64 file uploads**. Test against a **separate test experiment ID** before any real collection, since a trial run using this ID submits real files. Confirm 25 videos (one baseline plus 24 trials) and one CSV with 9 practice rows, 1 baseline row, 24 rated trial rows, 1 attention-check row, and 1 honesty-question row for a completed participant; for an intentionally interrupted test, confirm that a `.partial.json` backup appears after DataPipe recovers the session. A 202 response means DataPipe has queued the file, so it may appear in Google Drive later. Keep the Google Drive folder restricted to authorized researchers for identifiable face video; review consent and retention with your ethics team. The app keeps a common participant ID prefix on every filename and records each exact filename in the CSV.

## Study-design decisions to review

* Add-N follows the supplied Qualtrics timing. The response is captured at the 10-second deadline. The script generates four-digit numbers from 1000–9999 and applies mod-10 addition to each digit for Add-3; confirm that this is the intended scoring rule.
* The anagram bank is a working stimulus set. Some letters form other valid words; CAT/ACT, DOG/GOD, MAP/AMP and TEACHER/CHEATER are counted as correct. Norm difficulty and check all alternatives before data collection.
* The 60-press condition is a target, not a required minimum; total presses and time to reach the target (if reached) are saved. Pilot whether both button conditions reliably elicit the desired effort difference.
* Face detection can make mistakes, including false trial restarts. Treat restart counts as quality flags rather than an exclusion rule without validation. Run a browser pilot to confirm that the MediaPipe model and WASM assets load in your hosting environment and that different skin tones, glasses, and lighting are handled acceptably.

## Added participant questions

The attention check (after task trial 12) reads: “To show that you are reading this question, please select ‘Somewhat agree’ below.” Options: Strongly disagree; Somewhat disagree; Neither agree nor disagree; Somewhat agree; Strongly agree. The CSV saves `phase=attention_check`, `question`, `response`, and `attention_pass` (1 for Somewhat agree, 0 otherwise). There is no automatic exclusion or repeat.

The final question reads: “During the tasks, did you answer thoughtfully and try to follow the instructions?” Options: Yes; No. The CSV saves `phase=honesty_question`, `question`, and `response`.
